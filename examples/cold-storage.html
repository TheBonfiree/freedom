SERVICES.DOC

Six intake categories. Each begins with the same free assessment: we inspect the medium, report what is physically recoverable, and quote before any work starts.

[1] 8-inch, 5.25-inch and 3.5-inch floppy imaging

Flux-level imaging on calibrated drives, including hard-sectored and CP/M formats, and soft-sectored disks written by controllers that no longer exist. Output is a flux archive plus a filesystem extraction where one can be resolved.

[2] DAT, DDS and Exabyte tape

Cleaned transports, replacement drive heads and per-block retry. Audio DAT and data DDS are different problems and are quoted separately.

[3] Zip, Jaz, SyQuest and MO cartridges

Including click-of-death Zip cartridges, where the drive is the failure and the medium is usually intact.

[4] LTO-1 through LTO-9

Generational reads across a maintained drive set, plus recovery from tapes written by backup software whose vendor is gone. Catalogue reconstruction where the index is lost.

[5] Failing hard drives

Head-stack replacement and platter transfer in a class-100 bench, for drives that still spin and drives that do not. We do not attempt a second entry on a platter set we have already opened without telling you first.

[6] Optical rot

CD-R and DVD-R with degraded dye layers, read at reduced speed across multiple drives and reconciled sector by sector.

Municipal survey, 1987. Thirty-four disks from a housing survey, stored in a damp basement. Twenty-nine imaged on the first pass; four after a bake; one lost to substrate separation. The city now has the only readable copy of its own survey.

Regional broadcaster, 1994–1998. Two hundred and twelve tapes with no surviving catalogue. We rebuilt the index from block headers and returned it as a spreadsheet alongside the audio, which is the part the client actually needed.

Architecture practice, 1993. Sixteen cartridges of drawings for a building now undergoing conservation. Fifteen read clean; the sixteenth needed a donor drive sourced from a collector in Utrecht.

University research set, 2004. Eight tapes written by a backup product discontinued in 2009. We read the raw blocks and wrote a parser for its container format; the parser is with the department, under their own licence.

INTAKE.TXT

Before you send anything

  • Stop using the medium. A drive that is failing gets worse every time it spins.
  • Do not run consumer recovery software against a disk you care about. It writes.
  • Do not freeze drives. That advice is from a different decade and it was wrong then.
  • If a disk is wet, keep it wet and tell us. Drying it is what destroys it.

What to send

  • The medium, packed so it cannot move inside the box.
  • Anything you know about what wrote it — machine, software, year, operating system.
  • What you actually need back. "Everything" is a valid answer but a slower one.

What comes back

  • The original medium, in the condition we received it, unless you tell us otherwise.
  • A flux or block-level archive, which is the thing that outlives us both.
  • Extracted files in a current format, with a manifest and checksums.
  • A written report on what was unreadable, and why.
